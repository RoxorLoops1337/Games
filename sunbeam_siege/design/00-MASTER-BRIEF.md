# NINE LIVES: Defend the Sunbeam (working title)

A cat-themed lane-defense deck-builder roguelike in the spirit of Monster Train. Browser game, single static folder, no build step.

## Core fantasy
You are a clowder of cats defending the Sunbeam, the warm patch of light on a cushion at the top of an old house. Every night, the Pests climb up from the garden: mice, rats, dogs, and the household horrors cats hate (vacuum, bath, spray bottle, cucumber, vet carrier, laser dot that cannot be caught). Hold the house for nine nights to keep the Sunbeam.

## Structural skeleton (borrowed principles, our own skin)
- The house has 3 floors: Cellar (bottom), Parlor (middle), Attic (top). Above the attic sits the Sunbeam, the player's health ("Sunbeam Warmth").
- Enemies spawn in waves at the bottom and climb one floor per turn. When a wave reaches the top and its attack resolves, it burns Warmth.
- The player plays cards each turn: Cat units (placed on a floor, fight enemies on that floor), Spells (instant effects), Treats/Toys (room upgrades attached to a floor).
- Energy ("Zoomies") per turn, hand draw, discard, exhaust, just like a deck builder.
- Each run: pick a main clan and a side clan, a starting Champion (a special unit card that can be upgraded 3 ways during the run), then travel a branching map of 9 nights across 3 acts with battles, elites, shops, events, rest nooks, and bosses.
- Between fights: shops, events, card upgrades (Scratching Post), relics, card removal, and clan-specific reward draft.
- Meta: unlockable clans, champions, relics, and a difficulty ladder ("Mischief Rank").

## Design pillars
1. "One more night." Every run should produce at least one broken-feeling loop that the player discovers and feels clever about.
2. Cats, wholeheartedly. Every mechanic should be a cat behavior: nap, knock things off shelves, zoomies at 3am, sit in any box, kneading, purring, hairballs, hunting, pouncing, ignoring you.
3. Stacking everywhere. In-fight stacks (statuses that grow turn by turn), and run-long stacks (cards, champions, and relics that get permanently stronger).
4. Readable at a glance. Floors, enemy intents, and numbers must be clear on desktop and phone.
5. Charming and a bit dry in tone. Puns welcome, never cringe. No em dashes in any text.

## Candidate twists (the design agents must evaluate, tune, lock or reject)
- NINE LIVES: every cat unit has lives pips. When it dies it spends a life and returns to the discard pile or hand stronger or weaker instead of vanishing. Lives are a run-long resource that can be spent, traded and boosted.
- KNOCK IT OFF: certain cats push enemies down a floor, to delay their climb, and enemies landing on a lower floor take "Shatter" damage.
- BOXES: "If it fits, I sits." A floor can hold a Box; units placed in a Box gain bonus effects and count as hidden for one turn.
- THE NIGHT CLOCK / 3AM ZOOMIES: every 3rd turn is a Zoomies Turn with extra energy but cats act randomly in part, or a special phase.
- NAP: a cat that skips acting for a turn gets Rested and benefits next turn. Napping stacks.
- CUDDLE PILE: adjacent cats on the same floor connect; pile size gives bonuses.
- CATNIP SURGE: burst resource with a crash afterwards.
- YARN: tangle enemies, chain cards together.
- HAIRBALLS: stackable damage/debuff item created by cats and coughed up.
- PURR: stacking aura that heals and powers up the floor.

## Tech constraints
- Plain HTML + CSS + JavaScript, no frameworks, no build step, no external network assets. Must also work when opened from file:// so use classic scripts (not ES modules) loaded in order from index.html.
- Graphics are generated by code: procedural pixel-art style cats and enemies drawn on canvas or built as SVG, CSS animation, particles. No external image files required.
- Audio is synthesized with Web Audio (SFX plus a simple procedural music loop). Must be mutable.
- Saves in localStorage, wrapped in try/catch.
- Responsive: playable on a phone in portrait and on desktop.
- Data-driven: cards, enemies, relics, events are plain JS data objects in data files, plus a small effect engine. Aim for numbers: 200+ cards, 60+ enemies, 80+ relics, 40+ events. Quality over padding, but ambition is the point.
