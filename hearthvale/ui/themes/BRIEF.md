# Brief for every interface style designer

## Why
The owner of Hearthvale (a cozy co-op 3D village builder, mobile first) says: "the buttons don't look touchable enough. I want them to look and
feel way more like a real high-end mobile game." Today's HUD (the Classic style) is flat-ish paper and candy. We are making TEN complete interface styles,
each built with a DIFFERENT technique, and the owner will play them in the real game and pick. Your style must make every button look like a physical,
tempting thing you want to press: real depth, believable light and material, a satisfying pressed state, and a little life.

## What you deliver (only these files, nothing else)
- `hearthvale/ui/themes/<id>.css`: the style. EVERY rule scoped under `html[data-ui="<id>"]` (never leak). Override the base `style.css` by specificity.
- optional `hearthvale/ui/themes/<id>.js`: `export default function init({ root, id }) { ...; return dispose }`. Runs when the style is selected; use it to GENERATE ASSETS
  at runtime (canvas textures, WebGL-rendered sprites, SVG filter defs injected into the page, CSS custom properties holding data URIs) and to add
  pointer feedback (ripples, tilt, sound is NOT yours). It must clean up in dispose (remove injected nodes and listeners). Must be fast (< 300 ms) and never throw.
- optional generated binary assets under `hearthvale/ui/themes/<id>/` (png/webp/svg), total <= 400 KB. Runtime-generated assets are preferred.
- `hearthvale/ui/themes/<id>.md`: 6 lines: name, the technique, what makes it feel touchable, performance notes, known weaknesses.
- Screenshots for your report (see Verify). In `js/ui/theme.js` your style is already registered; do NOT edit it or any other file (index.html, style.css, js/*, shared/*, art/*).

## The fixed markup (you cannot change it, only style it)
The interface markup is generated by the game (js/ui/*.js and index.html). READ `hearthvale/style.css` (the Classic style, about 70 KB: your overrides build on it) and
the static scene snapshots `hearthvale/lab/ui_scenes/*.html` (real markup: hud, banner, build, building, valley, villagers, inventory, settings, levelup) to learn the classes.
Components you must restyle, at minimum: all buttons `.b` (variants `.b-green .b-gold .b-blue .b-pink .b-red .b-lilac .b-cream`, sizes `.big .small-b`, `.round`, states `:active`, `.off`,
`.shine`), round buttons `.rb` (and `.rb.alt`), the dock `#dock` with the hero `#bBuild`, tool buttons `#tools .rb`, pips `.pip`, `#quick` (Work!) and `#soupAlert`, the player card `#card`
(avatar ring, xp bar), wallet pills `.pill` (coin and scroll), resource chips in `#strip`, quest tracker `#track`, toasts, banners (trophy/quest), the sheet frame `#sheet`
(`.sh-head`, `.sh-title`, close button `.x`, `.sh-tabs` and `button.tab`, `.sh-body`), cards (`.card`, build cards with thumbnails), list rows (`.rowi`, `.vrow`), `.bar` progress tubes
(`.bar i`, colour variants `.g .bb .p .l .r`), `.stepper`, segmented `.seg`, switches `.sw`, range sliders, `.sec` section headers, `.badge-new`, `.ov-card` (level-up) with `.lvlbadge`,
modal dialogs and the settings style picker `.uis`. Keep layout, sizes and positions essentially as they are (tap targets >= 44 px, nothing overlapping, text readable). You may change
colours, shapes, borders, shadows, gradients, textures, filters, fonts (system font stacks only, no downloads), transitions and animations, and add pseudo-elements.

## The bar
Look at screenshots of Brawl Stars, Clash Royale, Hay Day, Genshin Impact, Candy Crush, Animal Crossing Pocket Camp, Marvel Snap, Royal Match, Monument Valley in your memory and web searches: what
makes their buttons feel like objects? Typically: a visible THICKNESS (dark under-edge or true extrusion), a specular highlight band, an inner glow, subtle gradient and noise or pattern, an outline
or rim light, a deep soft shadow that tightens when pressed, a confident chunky typographic treatment (stroked or embossed text), icons that sit on the surface with their own shadow, and a
pressed state that physically moves down and darkens. Do research (WebSearch/WebFetch) on your technique, and put 6 bullets of what you learned as a comment at the top of your CSS.
Every style must feel consistent across ALL scenes, and distinct from the other nine (see the list in js/ui/theme.js).

## Constraints
- Mobile performance: no more than a handful of `backdrop-filter` surfaces at once (never on long lists), avoid animating box-shadow blur on many elements, SVG filters only on small elements,
  prefer transforms and opacity for motion. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and `html[data-reduce="1"]` (disable your decorative animation there).
- The game sets `.lowfx` on body on low quality: provide a cheaper variant (`html[data-ui="<id>"] body.lowfx ...`) that drops expensive effects.
- Light and dark: the UI sits over a bright pastel 3D world; keep contrast and legibility (text over buttons >= 4.5:1 where it is real text).
- Never use an em dash character anywhere in files you write (comments, docs, strings): use commas, colons or hyphens.
- No git commands. The machine is small and nine other designers work at the same time: take ONE screenshot at a time, no long loops, close browsers.
