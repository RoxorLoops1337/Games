Running critique log for the polish loop. Two critics play the build, grade it and
issue demands; the build agents work through them; the critics re-grade. Whatever
survives a round lands here as **OPEN ITEMS** and becomes the next round's brief.

**Do not treat this file as gospel about the code** — it is a snapshot of what two
reviewers wanted at one moment. Read the game first, then this.

Hard rules the loop must never break (they are also in the workflow brief):
one self-contained file, exactly one `<script>` block, no external resources, no
renamed element ids, no change to the `window.BB` surface, and **no change to
balance or rules** — the FOES table, `breed`/`geneRoll`, `deriveCost`, `power`,
`MAX_PEN`, `MAX_DECK`, `SPILL`, `feedFor`, rituals, hazards and trait effects are
tuned by simulation. Looks and feel only.

## Visual design — 6/10
> Real progress — the arena has a floor, the loss screen is red, the type scale exists, the cards have thickness, the mobile Nest header no longer shreds, and the Wild screen is centred. Eight of my twelve demands landed. But the two things a stranger would notice first are still broken: your antagonist is an operating-system emoji (the final boss of the game is 🥚) sitting in a beautifully lit arena next to hand-drawn creatures, and your procedurally-bred beasts all have the same silhouette — ten cards on the Cull screen are ten recoloured ellipses with two stick legs, which means the gene system, the soul of the game, is invisible in the one place it should be undeniable. On top of that the framed pens you built are mostly empty: the Cull deckwrap is 660px tall around a 340px block of cards, and the orphan-row problem I flagged last time is untouched — a 2-card row against 700px of nothing.

Right now it's a 120px clipart rooster rendered in Segoe UI Emoji, floating above a floor you built specifically to make it feel present — and the finale, THE UR-MOTHER, is a beige egg icon. It reads as placeholder art next to your own canvas beasts, and it makes the whole screen look unfinished. 📋 and 🏦 have no silhouette and no menace at all.
      - *how:* You already have the machinery: `drawBeast()` proves canvas creatures work in this file. Add `drawFoe(cv, foe)` and replace `<div class="em" id="fEm">` with `<canvas id="fCv" width="520" height="400">` at CSS size `clamp(160px,28vw,260px)`. Give each foe a `sil` key selecting one of ~6 procedural silhouettes built from the same primitives drawBeast already uses (ellipse/quad/rect + a dark outline stroke at lineWidth 3 and a top-lit rim): `bird` (chicken), `brute` (ogre, bull, champion), `human` (Merle, Inspector, Vet, Repo Man), `swarm` (poachers, rustlers — three overlapping small brutes), `fire` (stacked flame quads, hue-cycled per frame), `egg` (Ur-Mother: a huge cracked ovoid with the pink eye-cluster). Keep the existing `.foe .em` transforms by moving `breathe`/`shake`/`windup`/`foelunge`/`foedie` onto the canvas element — zero animation rework.
- [ ] **Widen the gene vocabulary — the beasts are ten of the same blob.**
      - *why:* Look at the Cull screen: ten creatures, and the only differences a player can name are hue, eye count and horn count. `drawBeast` builds every animal from one ellipse (`rx`/`ry` vary by only ±38%), the same two 6×10px legs, the same wing ovals, the same three-tooth mouth. So the sentence 'a chick visibly resembles both parents' is technically true and visually inert — you cannot tell a Bulwark from a Feral at a glance, and the resemblance lines on the Hatch screen point at differences too small to see. This is the one mechanic nothing else on the market has, and it is currently the least legible thing on screen.
      - *how:* Add three genes with real silhouette authority and inherit them the same way the others are: `shape` (0–3 → ellipse / pear with a wide base / vertical capsule / lumpy blob drawn as 3 overlapping ellipses), `limb` (0–2 → stubs / long thin legs at 18 units / no legs, body sits on the ground with a squash), `crest` (0–3 → none / spines along the top arc / a floppy comb quad / antennae). Also give the horns real variance: taper length `8 + 14*v.hornLen` instead of the fixed `12*sz`. Then push contrast inside the body: a top-light band `ell(0, cy+ry*0.55, rx*0.8, ry*0.28, 'hsl(hue,55%,66%)')` and a 2.5px `dark` outline on legs and wings too, not just the body. Six shapes × 3 limbs × 4 crests is 72 silhouettes before hue — that is the difference between a colour swatch and an animal.
- [ ] **Stop centring cards in a giant empty frame. Fit the frame to the herd.**

The Book does the same with one row inside a 340px frame. The Wild screen on mobile centres a lone third card under a 2-column row. It reads as a layout that lost its content.

(1) `.deckwrap > .grid{ align-content:start }` and `.deckwrap{ flex:0 1 auto; max-height:100% }` so the frame shrink-wraps the herd — a 10-card barn should produce a frame ~360px tall, not 660px. ## Game feel — 6/10
> The build agents did the work. The turn is a sequence now, beasts lunge and flinch and fall, the sound layer has real voices instead of one square wave, the eggs crack one at a time with a drumroll, the cull prints a receipt, the resemblance threads fire, PRODIGY means something, numbers count and bump. Eight of my twelve landed properly. What is left is a different class of problem: the game now HAS beats and then throws them away. The turn drain hard-caps itself at 1150ms and self-compresses to a 130ms-per-beast blur exactly when the pen is full and the turn is worth watching. The killing blow — the thing the whole fight is for — is recorded as `killed:true` and never read; the foe just stops and then tips over 380ms later to four beige particles. And the ring itself is broken furniture: a third of the fight screen is empty black, the foe is a 72px emoji on a phone, and the hand cards are physically LARGER than the animals in the pen on mobile, which is the exact regression of demand #11 I already made once. Fix the timing budget, give the kill a moment, and rebuild the ring's proportions and this is a 7.5.
### CRITICAL
- [ ] **Stop compressing the turn into mush at the exact moment it matters.**
      - *why:* SEQ_BUDGET=1150 divides the whole transcript by its own length. A full pen (4 strikes 230 + foe 380 + swing 190 + hit 170 + die 340 = 2000ms of intent) scales to sc=0.575, so each strike beat fires every 132ms while the lunge animation itself runs 230ms and foeHit's shake runs 320ms. Four lunges, four white flashes and four 1000ms damage flyers all overlap. The player is back to seeing one smear of damage — the exact failure I named last pass, now dressed in animation code. It gets WORSE the better your board is, which is backwards.
      - *how:* Raise SEQ_BUDGET to ~2400 and add a floor: `const beat = Math.max(110, BEAT[e.k] * seq.sc)`. Never let a 'strike' beat land shorter than its own animation (230ms). Instead of scaling every beat equally, collapse the CHEAP ones first — drop 'draw'/'foeheal' to 0 and keep strike/die/you at full length. Add a hard skip: the END TURN button already goes .busy, so a long turn is already interruptible. Also stack the flyers: offset each successive flyer on the same target by +14px in Y and shorten `fly` to 700ms so four numbers read as four numbers, not a pile.
- [ ] **Give the killing blow a moment. It is the whole point of the fight.**

The foe's HP hits 0 mid-drain, the sequence keeps politely playing out, then finishSeq waits 380ms and resolveFight drops eight beige particles and a four-note arpeggio. The most important frame in the loop is indistinguishable from a normal hit.
      - *how:* In applyEv, when `e.killed`: skip the rest of the queue, hold on a 140ms freeze (add class `.app.stop{filter:contrast(1.35) saturate(1.4)}` and pause the drain), fire a heavier impact — `noise(0.03,{hp:200,vol:0.16})` + a downward `tone(180,0.5,{type:'sawtooth',glide:50})` + `duck(0.5,500)` — then let the foe's .dying play with 20+ particles in ITS hue rather than #ffd0a0, and slam a 'DOWN' banner with the existing `.banner.slam`.       - *how:* Draw the egg, do not borrow it: a canvas or a CSS ellipse in `--gold`/#e8d9a8 with a dark rim, matching `.card` width so the swap has no size jump. Keep the card at opacity 0 until 120ms into the crack and fade it up over 180ms as the halves clear, so the order reads shell → gap → chick. Scale the shell to the card (`width:100%; height:100%`) instead of a fixed 104px font that overflows the 150px mobile card.
- [ ] **Break the name collapse. By generation 6 the whole barn is called Fenmaw.**
      - *why:* `childName` picks `pre` from a-or-b and `suf` from a-or-b. After three generations of in-barn breeding every animal shares the same two syllable halves, so the run log reads 'Fenmaw the Damp out of Fenmaw the Second-Best x Fenmaw the Unwell' and the cull screen is nine identical words. You cannot form an attachment to an animal you cannot name apart from its siblings, and attachment is the entire emotional engine of a culling game.
      - *how:* Mutate a syllable when both parents match: if `a.pre === b.pre`, roll a fresh `pick(SYL_A)` ~50% of the time (this is a bloodline drifting, which is thematically correct). Widen TITLES and make the epithet earned rather than random — tie it to the chick's tags and stats ('the Wide' for high hide, 'the Quick' for swift, 'the Ninth' for a ninth-generation line). Make `uniquifyName`'s roman numerals a last resort, not the norm.
- [ ] **The prize animal cannot fight. Your trophy is a sandbag.**
      - *why:* The end screen shows 'Best in show: Fenmaw the Damp, gen 6, power 31.5' — Might 2, Hide 45. `power()` weights hide at 0.6 with no floor on might, and `geneRoll` compounds multiplicatively, so a line bred for one stat runs away to absurdity while the other stays at its starting value. The animal you spent a whole run selecting for is useless in the ring AND becomes next run's founder. The compounding fantasy pays out in a number that does nothing.
      - *how:* Two changes, both single-constant. Give power() a balance term: `p -= Math.abs(might - hide*0.5) * 0.4` so a lopsided freak scores below a rounded one. And cap the runaway in geneRoll by pulling the mean toward the pair average as values grow — multiply the mut band by `1/(1+base*0.012)` so gains diminish at high values. A 45-Hide monster should be a rare event, not the inevitable end state of any focused line.
### MINOR
- [ ] **Stop showing me a cull screen when nothing must be culled.**
      - *why:* `goCullOrNext()` fires `goCull()` whenever `R.deck.length > 4`, which is every round from round two onward. Twelve rounds means eleven trips through a full-screen grid whose header says 'THIN THE HERD IF YOU LIKE' and whose only action is tapping KEEP THEM ALL. That is a mandatory dead beat between the clutch payoff and the next fight — the exact place where 'one more round' momentum should be strongest.
      - *how:* Only route to goCull() when `mustCull() > 0`. Put optional culling behind a small button on the nest screen instead ('THIN THE HERD 10/10'), where the player is already making roster decisions. When a cull IS forced, keep the ceremony you built — the receipt is genuinely good and it is currently drowned by ten voluntary visits that have no receipt at all.

## Known compromises the build agents flagged themselves

- The pen canvas is 1.46:1 while a beast's natural silhouette is roughly square, so
  there is unavoidable side space inside each pen portrait.
- The turn-drain budget self-compresses to ~130ms per beast when the pen is full —
  exactly when the turn is worth watching. Both critics called this out.
- `#nBest` (live best-power readout) is hidden below 420px; there is no room for it.
- A streak payout in Slop was deliberately **not** built: Slop values are tuned
  balance and a payout would change the economy. Any streak reward must be cosmetic
  unless the balance is re-simulated.
